# Revised package: 15 October 2026

Status: local regression-tested candidate for client rehearsal. Live account connectivity and final client-demo acceptance remain outstanding.

Validation: 278 tests passed on Python 3.12; Python compilation completed successfully. Runtime versions are recorded in requirements.txt; test dependency in requirements-dev.txt. No new lint, coverage or vulnerability-scan claims are made.

Package contents: source, fixtures, tests and docs only. Local repository metadata (.git), the runtime run database (market_intelligence_runs.db) and the operations log (market_intelligence_operations.log) are not included; the database and log are recreated when the app runs.

Corrected (15 October, usability): the news window defaults to one month (30 days) instead of 7 days. Incident filters in the Operational Impact Center sit on their own row with a label above each box (Priority, Event type, Action, Demand basis, FDA class, Source), so values are no longer cut off; long filter values are shortened ("No store match" instead of "Not assessed - no Store match"), while the table keeps the full wording. In a weather incident's detail (Ajith's review: the selected incident and store went out of sight while scrolling), the incident name and its store/product/DC filters sit in a bar that stays pinned at the top of the screen while the detail is scrolled, and each section (replenishment, staffing, customer traffic) starts with a "Showing:" line naming the incident and the store selection it reflects. Notes under the replenishment tables and under the Demand Planner result are grouped in a collapsible "Notes and assumptions" block instead of loose paragraphs. The Configure page's BLS line states that CPI is US national and monthly and names the latest published month; a note in Advanced settings > Collector Tuning and on the Evidence and Export tab explains that CPI shows the newest month BLS has published, about two weeks after that month ends.

Corrected (15 October, Demand Planner logic): when a weather alert reached the selected store, the Demand Planner plans the same product categories the Operational Impact Center sized for that alert and store, so both tabs show the same products (previously the planner used the broader category list for the weather type). Recall category keywords now use product words only; recall-reason words (label, misbrand, mislabel) and a colour name no longer map food recalls to Household. Signals that are not product-mapped (headline and fuel CPI, retail news) state "Not calculated by design" with the reason, instead of reading like a missing calculation.

Corrected (14 October, product recalls): live recalls are matched to internal data in a fixed order -- exact UPC and lot, product name, product family (the recall counterpart of the weather event-family ladder), category, distribution. Priority is set only once the recall's UPC matches an internal item; otherwise it is left blank, the same rule as a weather alert with no store match. Lots are matched yes or no: a recall that lists no lot codes covers every lot of the UPC, and a recall whose lots are not in internal stock is "not affected". Withdrawal and POS block remain reserved for a UPC match. Synthetic inventory was added for three live recalls so exact matches can be demonstrated. The recall table no longer shows Distribution-Footprint Stores or Stores Carrying Possible Product Match; both remain in the incident detail.

Corrected (14 October, weather): a weather alert with no store in its area has a blank priority and Actionability "Monitor" in the Operational Impact Center and the Results tables. A store inside the alert area that is closed is shown as "matched, closed" rather than "no Store matched". For an alert that is already active, employee commutes are marked for confirmation instead of being compared with the time until the alert ends. NOAA wording separates alerts fetched from alerts retained for scoring.

Corrected (14 October, Demand Planner): weather store selection uses the stores an alert actually reached in the run; other stores in the same state are labelled as baseline context. Cards and calculations are unchanged. Store and product values (baseline forecast, on hand, inbound, available supply) are identical to the Operational Impact Center for every shared store and product.

Corrected (14 October, wording and export): plain data notice; "Why it may matter" and "Suggested next step"; "External risk indicators" naming the driving signal; "Illustrative Signal Scenarios"; Evidence Audit caption matches the relevance-ranked news selection; news described as retailer and retail-market news (competitors are not tracked); Business Risk Indicator, Source Confidence and Internal Exposure columns; an empty FDA search falls back to the default query; a non-US news market states that CPI, FDA and NOAA remain US sources; the Configure page shows Data Sources at full width with the run status line; CSV and JSON exports include Run ID, Retailer and the numeric Risk Score.

Earlier revision: 29 September 2026 (141 tests at that time). Its corrections follow.

Corrected (29 September, DC logical consistency): Primary DC route exposure is now shown as a planner warning rather than an automatic allocation blocker. Previously, if the alert county overlapped the Store or Dallas lane, Dallas could show ATP and a 4-10h transit lane but still show 0 planned supply, which looked contradictory in Ajith's review. Now, when the Primary DC has ATP and the approved lane can arrive before the alert deadline, Primary Planned Qty is populated; Route Risk remains visible in the action text so planners still validate the lane before execution.

Corrected (29 September): the Weather Incident Inbox's Recommended Action could name the Primary DC as the source of the full order quantity even when the DC Replenishment Plan had allocated that DC 0 units and sourced the shortfall from a backup DC instead -- two screens describing one sourcing decision two different ways. The Recommended Action now names each DC only for the units the replenishment plan actually assigns to it (Primary, then Backup, then an explicit escalation for anything still uncovered), and says plainly when no DC can supply anything rather than naming a DC that ships zero. Signal Search was also separated from the main intelligence run in this revision (Sumit): it is now a standalone Google Trends lookup with no scored signal feeding the main run, matching the earlier Apify-scope decision.

Corrected (29 September, follow-up audit): the Product Recall inbox's Action name collapsed to "Distribution footprint review" for three review states that are not distribution problems -- a lot-confirmation gap (lot_review_required) and a product/UPC-confirmation gap (product_review_required) both showed a distribution-review action while their own Reason text correctly described a lot or product-match problem, so the Action and Reason disagreed. Each of the five review states now gets its own distinct action name (Category recall review / Distribution footprint review / Lot confirmation review / Product/UPC confirmation review), matching what its Reason text actually says needs confirming. A stale line on the Configure page's hero copy that still described the main run as covering "search demand" (removed from the main run in the Signal Search separation above) was also corrected.

Corrected (29 September, simplification pass): the Store Demand & Inventory Impact and DC Replenishment Plan tables had accumulated enough new column names (Store Inventory As Of, Case Pack, MOQ, Primary/Backup ATP, Primary/Backup DC Supply, Route Risk) that the tables read as harder to follow than the version reviewed with Ajith, even though the underlying logic was correct throughout. Store Demand & Inventory Impact now shows Uplift % (renamed from Scenario Demand Assumption %) and Gap (renamed from Forecast Shortfall) as columns; Case Pack, MOQ and Store Inventory As Of move to a caption under the table, since the existing Quantity Rounding column already states the case-pack/MOQ rule in plain English. DC Replenishment Plan collapses Primary DC / Primary ATP / Primary DC Supply / Backup DC / Backup ATP / Backup DC Supply / Route Risk into three columns -- Serving DC, Available Qty, Planned Qty -- plus the existing Remaining Gap and Action, with Route Risk folded into the Action sentence rather than a separate Yes/No column. Serving DC names only the DC(s) actually shipping units, never a DC that ships zero, which keeps Ajith's own product-level requirement (actual serving DC, available quantity, planned quantity, per Store/Product) intact and demonstrates his 23 September single-sourcing example directly: a 100-unit requirement that only one alternate DC can fully cover is sourced entirely from that DC, and that DC alone is named. New regression tests pin this against the exact 23 September transcript example and the live zero-both-DCs case from WX-2FF6E59B.

Corrected (29 September, same pass): the sidebar Credentials panel (NVIDIA/Apify/BLS key fields) was gated behind the HIDE_ADMIN_CONTROLS environment variable and could silently disappear on one machine and not another depending on unrelated environment state, with nothing on screen explaining why. It is now always shown, on every machine, with no .env file or environment variable required for it to appear; HIDE_ADMIN_CONTROLS still controls only the separate developer-facing Raw Data tab.
